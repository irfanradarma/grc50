# Research and editorial decisions

## The argument this deck should establish

Brief tool refresh and safeguards → Audit the AI → Audit with AI and human authority → useful programmes and systems → integrated AMS → continuous risk assurance through SIGNAL.

AI is not presented as a substitute for accountability, an automatic increase in audit quality, or a source of objectively correct risk scores. The material follows the decision chain: purpose → evidence → interpretation → review → action → observed result.

## Material replaced

- **Wolters Kluwer webinar poll:** Replaced in the main narrative with RSM research and an explicit methodology; a self-selected vendor-webinar sample is weaker for the business transformation claim.
- **IIA/AuditBoard AI-fraud readiness statistic:** Removed from the main flow because AI-enabled fraud preparedness is narrower than general readiness to audit AI.

## Claims intentionally avoided

- No study is presented as proof that AI improves audit accuracy.
- International surveys are not described as Indonesian market estimates.
- Air Canada is not described as a proven LLM or generative-AI failure.
- NIST, ISO, The IIA and COSO are not interchangeable or a single combined standard.
- Case figures and matrix scores remain illustrative.
- No-training, retention, processor access, residency and connectors are distinct controls. The sweet-spot chart is an illustrative decision aid, not measured security or RSM policy.
- The first-section data module explains everyday use of web chatbots and CLI agents, not development of an AI platform. Local execution, file-read permissions and hosted inference are separate questions.
- The local AMS connects issued audit findings to the Aruna risk workspace. Prepared recommendations are not live model output.
- The idea-execution-usage graphic is a conceptual argument, not a quantified before/after study.
- Model-improvement opt-out, retention, processing location and permission to share remain distinct.
- A no-training control is not a sufficient condition for uploading confidential records.

## Primary-record checks

- RSM methodology: survey fieldwork 5–16 March 2026; 827 U.S. and 203 Canadian respondents; participating organisations already used AI, including pilots.
- The IIA AI framework, PDF p. 11: three domains—Governance, Management and Internal Audit.
- NIST AI RMF 1.0, p. 20 and function descriptions: GOVERN is cross-cutting.
- GIAS: selected standards referenced by number and paraphrased; the full standards remain the authority.
- PCAOB July 2024 Spotlight, pp. 4–5: staff observations and human supervision, not a current rule.
- Air Canada primary decision: 14 February 2024, 2024 BCCRT 149; facts at para. 2, accountability analysis at paras. 24–31 and order at para. 44. No model architecture is established.

## Source access

Official public sources were preferred over summaries, marketing statistics without methodology, and unattributed predictions. Some official sites were unavailable to the web-text tool; RSM’s report page and the tribunal’s embedded decision were independently read from their original public HTML/PDF endpoints. No paywall or credential barrier was bypassed.
